# Chinese and Chinese-market premium patterns

These patterns come from observing publicly reachable official sites. Translate
them into reusable decisions; do not borrow another brand's imagery or symbols.

## 1. Cultural heritage as evidence

Examples: 故宫博物院, 敦煌, 茅台.

Use material finishes, seals, mineral pigments, calligraphic rhythm, museum-like
restraint, and symbolic marks as evidence of cultural continuity because they
carry authority and can safely coexist with modern digital content. If the site
is available in another language, keep the meaning clear and let the inherited
texture carry the craft rather than translating it into a decorative cliché.

### Reusable lesson

Heritage is strongest when treated as substance and palette, not as a festival
decoration on top of a generic page.

## 2. Product-led technical trust

Examples: 华为, 腾讯, 小米, 比亚迪, 理想, 蔚来.

Use near-white or near-black spaces, one disciplined primary color or
spectacular image, obvious grid, and clear sequence of product, capability, and
proof. On product-led brand sites and usage-driven applications, descriptive
clarity and trust-tiered typography usually beat decorative play. Let one accent
carry the brand and reserve the remaining color for data or states.

### Reusable lesson

For high-volume or long-line sets, one color and one consistent grid do more to
create authority than a custom color per category.

## 3. Controlled marketplace density

Examples: 淘宝, 天猫, 苏宁, 拼多多, 京东, 百度.

In dense Chinese marketplaces, use a calm neutral base plus one heat accent in
near-neutral tones. Group risk areas like search, trust, and channels; make
generic noise non-monochrome and minimal so the primary lane reads first. Price
and trust badges are usually the strongest visual anchors.

### Reusable lesson

When content must be dense, make the container feel trustworthy and use one or
two accents to control the eye, not a fresh accent every module.

## 4. Sport and fashion confidence

Examples: 李宁, 安踏, FILA 中国站, 波司登, PEACEBIRD.

Use angular geometry, climate and material texture, burst breadcrumbs,
category-led imagery, and clear product groupings. It can work with both
inherited craft and trendy youth expression if both obey the same grid. When
several languages or slogans exist, let one primary visual carry story and the
other repeat silently.

### Reusable lesson

Youth expression can feel confident without being decorative chaos; product
categories and one shared visual grammar are the stabilizers.

## 5. Contemporary lifestyle imagery

Examples: 小红书, 毛戈平.

Use lived-in texture, light, skin, food, space, and short narrative clubs to make
modern Chinese consumer aesthetics feel social rather than institutional.
Photographs can be bright and human but should still leave room for the brand
system to stay recognizable. A single warm accent is often enough.

### Reusable lesson

Contemporary Chinese lifestyle design rewards warmth from photographed evidence;
do not manufacture it with unrelated gradients or busy borders.

## Palette anchors

Use one anchor family in a row and at most one of these as the main accent:

1. `Ink + cream + gold` — readable, formal, and museum-like.
2. `Vermilion + ceramic white` — strong for heritage or celebratory moments.
3. `Mineral blue or jade + near-black` — calm, technical, and modern.
4. `Neutral grey + one heat accent` — good for marketplaces and product suites.
5. `Near-white + soft black + human warmth` — good for beauty or lifestyle.

## Bilingual and local text checks

1. Check one pure-Chinese, one mixed Chinese-English, and one punctuation-heavy
   headline for length and line break behavior.
2. Check whether a block needs full-width punctuations and visibly different
   font spacing, not just translated copy.
3. Verify that inherited cultural symbols and warm accents do not hide the main
   action or make the product range look less credible.
